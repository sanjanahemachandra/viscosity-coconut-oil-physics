# Investigating the Viscosity of Coconut Oil

## Overview
An individual work-home experiment carried out as part of **PH1023 - General 
Physics Laboratory II** at the Faculty of Science, University of Colombo. 
Viscosity of coconut oil was determined using a modified falling sphere method, 
applying Stokes' Law to clay balls of varying radii falling through the oil column.

## Objectives
- Measure terminal velocities of clay balls of different radii in coconut oil
- Calculate the viscosity coefficient of coconut oil using Stokes' Law
- Compare experimental results with standard values

## Materials & Tools
- Transparent container (~75 cm height) filled with coconut oil
- Plasticine clay balls (radii: 2 mm, 3 mm, 5 mm, 6 mm)
- Smartphone video recording + Tracker software for motion analysis
- Excel for data tracking
- Python (Matplotlib) for graph plotting

## Method Summary
Clay balls of varying radii were dropped into coconut oil and recorded 
on video. Videos were analyzed using Tracker software to extract terminal 
velocities. A Terminal Velocity vs Radius Squared graph was plotted and 
the slope used to calculate viscosity via Stokes' Law.

## Key Results
- Terminal velocity increased with ball radius, consistent with Stokes' Law
- Calculated viscosity of coconut oil: **0.048 Pa·s**
- Standard value at room temperature: 0.03–0.04 Pa·s
- Results align reasonably well with theoretical expectations
